From Transit to Transformation: Trade-Driven Structural Transformation in Central Asia & the South Caucasus

A policy presentation delivered to the World Bank Group's Europe and Central Asia Vice Presidency — Territorial Development Team — as part of the Johns Hopkins SAIS Central Eurasia Practicum (April 2026). The analysis examines how Central Asian and South Caucasus countries can leverage trade and transport corridors to generate inclusive economic growth and jobs, identifying soft infrastructure modernization and regional coordination as the binding constraints on corridor performance. Prepared in collaboration with John Nickell, Nural Aliyev, and Zoha Noor.

Key Findings

  • Growth along existing corridors is capital-intensive with limited job creation, weak SME linkages, and persistent barriers for women and youth — requiring a structural shift from transit facilitation to value-chain integration.
  • Border-crossing improvements have been uneven across the region: countries that modernized individually saw gains offset by bottlenecks elsewhere, confirming that a corridor's speed is set by its slowest point, not by averaging strong performers.
  • Kazakhstan's KEDEN platform demonstrates what is achievable — cutting container processing from 3 hours to 30 minutes and vehicle processing from 9 hours to 30 minutes, while boosting customs revenue 16% — but success has not cascaded regionally due to sovereignty risk, adoption costs ($5–25M per country), and the absence of cross-border trust mechanisms.
  • The CPMM dataset (2011–2023) shows informal payments persisted at 24% despite individual-country improvements, highlighting that internal efficiency gains do not automatically produce regional impact without mutual recognition frameworks.
  • The team recommends four complementary pillars: AEO Mutual Recognition (regional trust framework), Customs Platform Modernization (KEDEN-model adoption), e-TIR Integration (UNECE cross-border transit standard), and Interoperability Standards (UN/CEFACT data backbone) — projected to reduce informal payments from 24% to 10–12% and processing times to under 30 minutes for compliant traders.
  • Armenia's TUMO digital-skills pipeline and Georgia's fintech ecosystem offer South Caucasus models with direct replication potential eastward into Central Asia, enabling inclusion without requiring countries to build new institutions from scratch.
